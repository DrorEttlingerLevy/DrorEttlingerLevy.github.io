Our research on using underwater sounds and artificial intelligence to monitor fish behavior was featured in **Ynet** in the article **"How do fish sound? Israeli researchers use AI to decode underwater feeding noises."**

[Read the full article on Ynet](https://www.ynetnews.com/environment/article/r1aq4imrbg)

The article presents our work on listening to the sounds produced by gilthead seabream while feeding and using these acoustic signals to better understand their feeding activity.

The idea is simple: instead of deciding how much to feed fish only according to biomass and predefined feeding tables, we can also **listen to the fish themselves**.

By combining passive underwater acoustic monitoring, signal processing, and machine learning, we can isolate characteristic feeding sounds from the noisy aquaculture environment and transform them into information about feeding behavior.

This could eventually allow farmers to monitor the response of the fish in real time and make better decisions about whether feeding should continue or stop, reducing both unnecessary feed costs and environmental impacts.

![Ynet coverage of our research](/images/publications/ynet.png)

## From Underwater Recordings to Feeding Information

Our experiments use hydrophones to continuously record the acoustic environment inside fish tanks.

![Underwater acoustic sampling](/images/publications/sampling.png)

The recordings contain not only fish sounds but also substantial background noise from water circulation, aeration, pumps, and other equipment. Signal-processing methods are therefore used to isolate the acoustic patterns associated with feeding.

## AI for Aquaculture Monitoring

The broader research combines acoustic sensing with AI to turn underwater recordings into quantitative information about fish behavior.

![AI-based fish monitoring](/images/publications/ynet_yolo.png)

The long-term goal is to develop non-invasive monitoring systems that allow aquaculture facilities to respond to the animals' actual behavior rather than relying only on predetermined feeding schedules.
