I presented my PhD research at the 54th Annual Conference for Science and Environment, organized by the Israel Society of Ecology and Environmental Sciences (ISEES), at Binyanei HaUma in Jerusalem.

The talk focused on how passive underwater acoustics can be combined with signal processing and machine learning to quantify fish feeding activity. By listening to the sounds fish produce while feeding, we can extract behavioral information that can help farmers better determine when to continue feeding and when to stop.

A central part of the talk was the transition from controlled experiments to a commercial aquaculture environment. Models that perform well in the laboratory must also cope with pumps, machinery, background noise, thousands of fish, and constantly changing environmental conditions in the field.

This work demonstrates how AI and acoustic sensing can contribute to precision aquaculture by improving feeding decisions, reducing unnecessary feed, and decreasing the environmental impact of intensive fish farming.
